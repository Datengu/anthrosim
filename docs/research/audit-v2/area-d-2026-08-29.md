## AV2-005 adversarial arithmetic — partial-supply condition loss

The v9 repair correctly partitions a reference-quarter maximum condition-loss quantity `R` over arbitrary M3 intervals. For `R = 100`, every allowed complete-year partition sums to exactly `4R = 400` maximum-loss points.

However, the current under-supply update computes:

```text
deficit = 1000 - supplied_permille
loss_numerator = deficit * interval_max_loss
loss = ceil(loss_numerator / 1000)   when numerator > 0
```

The ceiling is applied separately at every M3 boundary.

This means the exactly conserved maximum-loss budget does **not** imply an exactly conserved realized partial-supply response. Any positive fractional interval loss is promoted to at least one whole condition point at each settlement.

An independent integer-only checker is preserved at:

`docs/research/audit-v2/area-d-condition-rounding-audit.py`

It mirrors only the current interval-bound, reference-quarter allocation and per-boundary ceiling equations; it does not call or depend on the Rust implementation.

### Exact result for a constant 1-permille supply deficit

For `referenceQuarterMaxLoss = 100`:

| M3 settlements/year | Annual max-loss budget | Current realized annual loss |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 400 | 1 |
| 4 | 400 | 4 |
| 12 | 400 | 12 |
| 52 | 400 | 52 |
| 365 | 400 | 365 |

Across all allowed `P = 1..365`, the annual max-loss budget remains exactly 400, yet the current realized loss has 365 distinct values: exactly `1..365` for this constant 999/1000 supply case.

The unrounded annual response implied by the linear deficit rule is only `400 × 1/1000 = 0.4` condition points. Repeated interval ceilings therefore turn a sub-unit complete-year effect into as much as 365 authoritative condition points.

### Broader deficit table

Exact current arithmetic:

| Supply deficit (permille) | P=1 | P=4 | P=12 | P=52 | P=365 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 1 | 4 | 12 | 52 | 365 |
| 10 | 4 | 4 | 12 | 52 | 365 |
| 50 | 20 | 20 | 24 | 52 | 365 |
| 100 | 40 | 40 | 48 | 52 | 365 |
| 500 | 200 | 200 | 204 | 209 | 365 |
| 999 | 400 | 400 | 400 | 400 | 400 |
| 1000 | 400 | 400 | 400 | 400 | 400 |

The existing regression `condition_response_does_not_multiply_with_resource_partition` checks only the two endpoints that hide this problem:

- full supply, where elapsed recovery is applied directly; and
- zero supply, where deficit = 1000 and every interval's ceiling is exactly the interval maximum-loss quantity.

No current regression holds a nonzero partial supply fraction fixed while varying `P`.

## Finding

### AV2-005 — per-boundary ceiling bias multiplies partial-supply condition deterioration

Severity: **P1**

Issue: #326

The normative `m3-response-time-contract-v1.md` says a continuously applicable response has the same complete-year budget and that changing M3 partition cannot multiply annual condition recovery/loss merely by adding boundaries. It also explicitly says partial supply applies the supply-deficit proportion to the elapsed maximum-loss amount.

The per-boundary integer ceiling violates that contract for partial supply. Finer M3 settlement can greatly amplify condition deterioration while elapsed exposure, supply fraction and the configured response coefficient are otherwise held fixed.

This is scientifically consequential because condition feeds condition-mediated mortality and M4 condition pressure. The defect is therefore not a harmless one-unit rounding discrepancy; allowed temporal-resolution changes can produce hundreds of condition points of divergence.

## Deferred hypotheses / later audit areas

- **Temporal convergence:** finite capacity and evolving condition legitimately make trajectories depend on M3 settlement timing. After #326 is repaired, the magnitude and convergence of those declared resolution effects belongs to Area I rather than being assumed benign.
- **Cross-system condition path:** condition response is settled before condition-mediated mortality on each M3 boundary; the interaction of a dynamically evolving condition path with mortality resolution can remain partition-sensitive even after simple response-budget repair. Its convergence/interpretation should be tested under Areas I/N rather than conflated with the demonstrated ceiling artifact.
- **Observability integrity:** `ResourceSystem.period_observations` are downstream diagnostic history and deliberately excluded from the causal resource digest. Whether complete preserved analysis history is sufficiently integrity-bound and whether all warnings are interpreted correctly belongs to Areas K/L.
- **Household structure:** #324 can change resource-sharing units and therefore M3 outcomes, but that is the Area C structural finding and is not double-counted here.
