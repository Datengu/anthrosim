## Existing contracts that held under review

The current implementation has several important protections that were not contradicted by this audit:

- cross-household direct parent/child kin anchors are reciprocal;
- female/male parent roles are symmetric for the first-degree M4 kin proxy;
- all unique kin locations are retained rather than truncating to one record-order-dependent location;
- an explicit regression protects kin-location collection against person-record order;
- household fission emits authoritative events containing the exact `people_reassigned` PersonIds;
- fissioned household topology is extended into M9 state rather than leaving mismatched household-vector shapes;
- deterministic size fission obeys the configured living-member cap and balanced group sizes;
- the lifecycle treatment is checkpoint/resume deterministic;
- fissioned households are intentionally auditable independent future M9 participants.

These properties establish determinism and observability, but they do not by themselves make the fission membership rule scientifically neutral.

## Adversarial experiment 1 — PersonId relabelling of household composition

`Population::fission_oversized_households(...)` enumerates living members in ascending person-index / PersonId order. The source comment explicitly names stable PersonId order as the “neutral partition rule”. Balanced group sizes are then assigned as contiguous slices: the first slice remains in the source household and subsequent slices move to newly created households.

Model-born people are appended to persistent person storage, so later birth cohorts necessarily receive later PersonIds. The bookkeeping identity is therefore correlated with birth order and, over multiple generations, genealogical depth.

Disposable PR #323 constructs the same one-household population twice with the same eight ages `[60, 50, 40, 30, 20, 15, 10, 5]`, identical reproductive sex, residence and condition, with mortality/fertility/resource mortality/migration disabled. The only change is the consistent assignment of those ages to canonical PersonIds. Under `deterministic_size_fission_v1(4)`, one labeling leaves the four oldest people in the source household while the reversed labeling leaves the four youngest people there.

The clean pure-relabelling probe passed on audit head `344916d11919b9d67ee1b97249e5841a8fad825a`; CI quality job `99054685995` explicitly records:

`audit_probe_fission_household_composition_depends_on_person_id_relabelling ... ok`

Thus unlabeled household age composition can change solely because scientifically irrelevant bookkeeping IDs are reassigned.

## Adversarial experiment 2 — same-day births and newborn-only fission

The authoritative annual scheduler applies household lifecycle after the annual demographic finalizer. Newborns created at that boundary are appended at the tail of person storage and therefore receive the highest PersonIds.

The strengthened current-head #323 fixture uses one household containing four 25-year-old females and one 30-year-old eligible male, zero mortality, certain fertility, zero birth spacing, zero resource need/condition mortality, migration disabled, and `deterministic_size_fission_v1(5)`.

At day 365 four births should expand the household from five adults to nine living people. The balanced split is 5 + 4. Under contiguous PersonId slicing, IDs 1–5 remain in the source household and same-day newborn IDs 6–9 are assigned together to the new household.

Final CI confirmation of this exact current-head fixture was still pending when this report draft was first written. The finding below does not depend on the stronger fixture because the pure relabelling behavior is already executable-demonstrated; the newborn-only case will be promoted to demonstrated evidence in the final Area C ledger if the current-head test passes as predicted.

## Mobility consequence of child-only households

M9 household trigger eligibility has no adult/guardian requirement. A household is excluded for no living members, an active journey, residence inside the focal region, unreachable travel, or timing constraints; age/dependency composition is not an eligibility criterion. Fissioned households are deliberately extended into M9 topology and existing acceptance tests treat them as independent future participants.

M4 likewise evaluates/moves the household as the persistent unit rather than requiring an adult composition. Therefore a child-only or newborn-only household produced by lifecycle partition is not inert storage: it becomes an independent resource-sharing and mobility unit under enabled mechanisms.

## Finding

### AV2-004 — deterministic household fission derives social composition from PersonId / birth-append order

Severity: **P2**

Issue: #324

The structural-sensitivity alternative introduced for #207 is deterministic but not neutral with respect to scientifically arbitrary identity labels. Stable PersonId slicing creates implicit cohort/generation sorting, can separate parents from children as a consequence of storage order, and can create very young autonomous households whose later resource/movement behavior follows from that arbitrary partition.

This does not affect the historical/default fixed-founder household lifecycle. It affects the opt-in fission treatment used to test structural sensitivity, so P2 is appropriate rather than P0/P1. However, the treatment cannot safely stand in for a generic “plausible neutral household turnover” alternative until its membership semantics are repaired or explicitly redefined.

This matters for interpretation of #304: replicated analyses already show large differences between fixed and fission household structures, including substantially higher mate limitation and different realized growth under fission. Those experiments prove that lifecycle structure matters, but the effect size/direction of this specific fission arm should not be assumed to represent household turnover generally while the partition itself contains a birth-order artifact.

## Unresolved cross-system hypothesis

The annual lifecycle intentionally skips an oversized household when that household is temporarily away from residence. A short M9 absence that happens to overlap the annual lifecycle boundary can therefore defer otherwise-required fission until a later annual boundary. This may be a defensible scheduling rule, but its scientific consequences belong in the mandatory households × movement / cross-system integration pass rather than being silently classified during this subsystem audit.

## Scientific boundary

Neither `fixed_founder_v1` nor `deterministic_size_fission_v1` is an empirical prehistoric household model. Area C evaluates whether the executable structural alternatives obey their declared null/sensitivity meaning. Passing deterministic invariants cannot establish that a household lifecycle is historically realistic.
